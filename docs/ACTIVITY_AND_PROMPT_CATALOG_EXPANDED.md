## How At Long Last actually works — the design grammar

The catalog looks like 900+ prompts. Underneath it is a much smaller set of moves, recombined. Once you see the moves, every activity makes sense:

### 1. The five core moves
- **Report, don't fix:** Weather Report, Rose Thorn Seed, Invisible Backpack. The rule is disclosure without problem-solving. The game protects the speaker by making listening the win.
- **Make the invisible visible:** Small Mercies, Gratitude Snapshot, Wanted List, Favorite Detail. Specificity is the mechanic. Vague praise (“you're great”) is banned by design; the prompts force a moment, a detail, a sentence.
- **Seal it, then reveal it:** Vault, Same Wavelength, Mutual Yes, Letterpress, Our Next Line, Gratitude Duel. Privacy first creates honesty. The reveal creates payoff. The delay is the point — anticipation is a form of care.
- **Build a tiny world together:** House Rules, Build-A-Cult, Lore Drop, Museum of Us, Couple Mascot. You invent lore, rules, mascots, museums. Shared fiction is low-risk intimacy: you learn what your partner values by what they invent.
- **Move together, not just talk:** Tempo Tap, Dual-Axis Maze, Harmonic Lock, Kiss Courier, Heartbeat Hold, Blind Canvas. Non-verbal synchrony — rhythm, maze, drawing, breath — lets couples attune without needing the right words.

### 2. Intensity is not heat — it's vulnerability cost
- **Intensity 1:** Low disclosure, high play. You can answer in a sentence, laugh, and move on. Safe for first nights, tired nights, new couples.
- **Intensity 2:** Requires a memory, a preference, or a small admission. You have to choose to be seen.
- **Intensity 3 (Mutual Yes only):** Requires double-blind consent. The design does the hard part — no one ever learns about a one-sided yes — so a mutual yes feels safe and earned.

### 3. Type is the interaction, not the topic
`prompt` = talk. `journal` = write to keep. `ritual` = do a tiny repeatable thing. `vault` / `harmonic` / `photo` / `match` / `prediction` / `vibe-sync` / `tempo` / `word` / `maze` / `canvas` / `bluff` / `mutual-yes` = a special mechanic. Two activities can share a topic (e.g. gratitude) but feel totally different because the type changes the risk.

### 4. Why the same question appears in three banks
The Shared Night Choice Bank (50 questions) appears in Mind Meld, Prediction Box, and Wavelength *on purpose*. The frame changes the meaning:
- In **Mind Meld**, “A rainy afternoon calls for…” asks: *Do we match?*
- In **Prediction Box**, the same question asks: *Do you know me?*
- In **Wavelength**, it asks: *Can you answer for yourself AND guess my answer?*
Same words, three different relational tests. Don't deduplicate them — the repetition is how couples learn the difference between matching, knowing, and guessing.

### 5. The game's bias
Across all 76 activities, the bias is consistent: **ordinary over extraordinary, specific over grand, repair over perfection, consent over pressure.** Even the Spicy set is built on that bias — Replay, Rewind, Favorite Detail, and Green Light Tonight are all about tiny, specific, opt-in closeness. The Playful set uses absurdity to make honesty cheaper. The Tender set uses structure to make vulnerability cheaper.

## Board activities — Tender (24)


### Comfort Menu — `comfort-menu` · prompt · Intensity 1
**What it really does:** Turns “support me” from mind-reading into a usable menu. Couples fail at comfort not from lack of care but from guessing wrong — offering advice when the partner wanted company, or company when they wanted space. This activity externalizes preferences *before* a crisis.
**Why the prompts work:** They move from crisis (“overwhelmed,” “go quiet”) → low-energy logistics (“three-item menu,” “ten-minute reset”) → anticipation (“difficult morning,” “waiting for news,” “plans fall through”). Each prompt forces a verb: say / do / bring. That verb is the gift.
**Play it when:** Early in a night, or after a hard week. Pair with Gentle Signal — Comfort Menu is *what* helps, Gentle Signal is *how* to ask for it.
**Make it land:** Answer with one concrete thing, not a philosophy. “Bring tea, sit nearby, don't ask questions” beats “just be there.” **Watch out:** Don't debate each other's menus. If one partner wants space and the other wants closeness, both menus are true.
**Expansion — 4 new prompts:**
- When I'm overstimulated by noise, people, or screens, the most helpful reset is…
- What does comfort look like when you're sick — not sad, just physically wiped out?
- If I could only remember one sentence from your comfort menu on a bad day, what should it be?
- Design a comfort menu for *us* as a team on a day when we're both running on empty.
**Twist — Swap Menus:** Each partner writes the *other's* comfort menu from memory, then compares. The gaps are the real conversation.

### Small Mercies — `small-mercies` · prompt · Intensity 1
**What it really does:** Attention training. It fights negativity bias by forcing the brain to scan the week for tiny kindnesses that would otherwise evaporate. The key is “probably thought you did not notice” — it rewards *unseen* labor.
**Why the prompts work:** Every prompt is small, recent, and specific. “This week,” “lately,” “this month.” No grand lifetime praise. The last two prompts widen the lens — differences that helped, kindness to others — so it doesn't become transactional scorekeeping.
**Play it when:** When things feel fine but flat. Pair with Gratitude Snapshot — Mercies is spoken, Snapshot is photographed.
**Make it land:** Name the action, not the trait. “You remembered I had that call and texted after” > “You're thoughtful.” **Watch out:** Don't turn it into “you never…” This is not a correction tool.
**Expansion:**
- What is a tiny thing I did today that made your day 2% easier?
- Which of my boring, invisible tasks do you secretly appreciate?
- Name a kindness I offered that I didn't even realize was kind.
- What small mercy do you want to offer *yourself* this week, and how can I help?
**Twist — Mercy Receipt:** After sharing, each partner writes the mercy on a slip (or in chat) to keep. The physical receipt makes it last.

### Weather Report — `weather-report` · prompt · Intensity 1
**What it really does:** Externalizes inner state via metaphor so you can disclose without diagnosing. “I'm stormy” is easier and safer than “I'm angry at work and also at you and I don't know why.” The “no fixing, just reporting” rule is the whole mechanic.
**Why the prompts work:** They stay in the metaphor — season, temperature, sky, umbrella, forecast, advisory. That distance makes honesty possible. Later prompts add care without fixing: “sit beside a cloud,” “shelter us,” “gentle change of pace.”
**Play it when:** As an opener, especially when one partner is quiet. Pair with Rose, Thorn, Seed for a fuller check-in — Weather is *how I feel*, Rose/Thorn/Seed is *what happened*.
**Make it land:** Mirror, don't solve. “Stormy with a chance of clearing” → “Got it, stormy. I'm here.” **Watch out:** Don't translate the metaphor back into a problem to solve.
**Expansion:**
- If your weather had a wind speed today, what is pushing you around?
- What is one small thing that would be like sun breaking through for an hour?
- Describe our *shared* weather today — are we in the same system or different ones?
- What forecast do you want for us tomorrow, even if today was overcast?
**Twist — Two-City Forecast:** Each partner gives their own weather, then gives a forecast *for the other*. Compare — did you read each other right?

### Receipts — `receipts` · prompt · Intensity 1
**What it really does:** Uses photos as memory anchors, then forces co-narration. The race to find a photo is play; the story swap is intimacy. Photos bypass “what did we do?” and get to “what did it *mean*?”
**Why the prompts work:** Every prompt asks for what the photo *doesn't* show — sound, off-camera story, background detail, what happened on the way there. That gap is where the real memory lives. “Nearly deleted” and “ordinary day” prompts are the strongest — they sacralize the unremarkable.
**Play it when:** Long-distance or after time apart. Pair with Photo Flashback — Receipts is race + story, Flashback is dual captions.
**Make it land:** Let the photo-finder tell first. Don't correct their version — add yours. **Watch out:** Skip photos tied to a painful memory unless both partners want to go there.
**Expansion:**
- Find a photo where we both look tired but happy. What were we tired *from*?
- Find the most boring photo that still makes you smile. Why is it still on your phone?
- Find a photo you took *for* me but never sent. What stopped you?
- Choose a photo from before we met that explains something about you now.
**Twist — Exhibit Swap:** Each partner picks a photo *of the other* for the tiny museum, writes the exhibit card, then reveals.

### Future Artifact — `future-artifact` · journal · Intensity 2
**What it really does:** Materializes values. Choosing 3 objects for a box 50 years from now forces you to say what this relationship *is* — not in abstract values, but in things. Objects are easier to be honest about than feelings.
**Why the prompts work:** They alternate scales — 50 years, 1 year, next adventure, a shelf, a receipt, a drawer. “Prove we were a good team” and “worn-out object to repair” are the emotional core: they frame love as maintenance, not just magic.
**Play it when:** Anniversary-adjacent, or when planning a next chapter. Pair with Museum of Us — Future Artifact looks forward, Museum looks back.
**Make it land:** Describe the object *and* why it matters. A mug is not a mug if it was the first morning mug. **Watch out:** Don't make it a shopping list. “Make rather than buy” is there to keep it handmade.
**Expansion:**
- What object from *this week* deserves to go in the box, even though it seems too small?
- If future-us opened the box on a hard day, which object should be on top?
- Design an artifact for a milestone we haven't named yet — what would it celebrate?
- What object would you *remove* from the box because we've outgrown what it meant?
**Twist — Repair Shop:** Each partner picks one worn-out object (real or metaphorical) and describes how they'd repair it together.

### Postcard From Next Year — `postcard-next-year` · journal · Intensity 2 · Saves +12 months
**What it really does:** Creates delayed intimacy. Writing to future selves is low-risk because the audience is *us later*, not a judge now. The +12 month open date turns a small note into a ritual.
**Why the prompts work:** The 2-sentence constraint is the mechanic — brevity forces choice. Prompts cycle through memory (“Remember when we…”), hope, learning, pace, and pleasure. “Gentle reminder about taking life at our own pace” is the game's thesis in one line.
**Play it when:** End of a night, or start of a season. Pair with Growth Ring — Postcard writes forward, Growth Ring reads backward.
**Make it land:** Write it *together* out loud, then seal it. Reading it aloud makes it a promise, not homework. **Watch out:** Don't make predictions that become pressure. Keep it to hopes, not contracts.
**Expansion:**
- Write a postcard from a version of us who handled a hard thing well. What did we learn?
- Leave future-us one permission slip: what are we allowed to stop doing?
- What ordinary Tuesday do you hope still exists in a year?
- Write a 2-sentence toast for the *ordinary* version of us a year from now, not the milestone version.
**Twist — Postcard Reply:** When you open last year's postcard, each partner writes a 2-sentence reply: “You were right about…” / “You didn't know yet that…”

### Translation — `translation` · prompt · Intensity 1
**What it really does:** Decodes implicit communication. Every couple has a private dialect — “I need company” might sound like “I'm fine.” This activity makes the dialect explicit so you don't have to decode under stress.
**Why the prompts work:** They are all translations: “How I say I love you without saying it is…”, “When I ask for a quiet evening, I'm really hoping for…” The prompts name the gap between words and meaning and invite a glossary.
**Play it when:** After a misunderstanding, or when one partner says “you should just know.” Pair with Gentle Signal — Translation is the dictionary, Signal is the phrasebook.
**Make it land:** Offer your own translation first. “When I go quiet, I mean…” is more useful than “You never…” **Watch out:** Don't use this to litigate past misreads. It's for future clarity.
**Expansion:**
- How do you say “I'm proud of you” without using those words?
- What does “I need a minute” mean in your language — 5 minutes, an hour, or just a breath?
- Translate this for me: when I say “whatever you want,” what am I actually hoping you'll do?
- What is one phrase of mine you wish I would retire because it lands wrong?
**Twist — Live Translation:** For the next 10 minutes, one partner narrates their inner translation out loud: “When I said X, I meant Y.”

### Map Pin — `map-pin-wish` · ritual · Intensity 1
**What it really does:** Maps desire spatially. “Where would you go?” is easier than “What do you want?” Places carry memory, fantasy, and values without needing to name them. Guessing *why* is the intimacy move.
**Why the prompts work:** They move from wish → hometown tour → tiny walk → childhood place → picnic → sunrise → ordinary-become-special. The “halfway between dream destinations” prompt is negotiation disguised as geography.
**Play it when:** When planning feels heavy. Pair with Date That Never Ends (Spicy) — Map Pin is the pin, Date That Never Ends is the itinerary.
**Make it land:** Describe what you'd *see* from the blanket, not just the city name. Sensory detail makes a pin feel real. **Watch out:** Don't turn it into a real trip plan with budget and logistics. Keep it wishful.
**Expansion:**
- Drop a pin where you'd go if you had one hour, no phone, and each other.
- Place a pin for a place that *only* makes sense to us — no tourist would go there.
- Where would you put a pin for “the place we argue best and repair fastest”?
- Choose a pin for a place you want to *return* to, but as different people than last time.
**Twist — Blind Pin:** One partner describes a place with 3 clues, the other has to guess the city before the reveal.

### Director's Commentary — `director-commentary` · prompt · Intensity 1
**What it really does:** Re-authors shared history as story. Telling “the day we met like a movie trailer” lets you choose what mattered, what was funny, and what the camera lingers on. Narrative control is agency.
**Why the prompts work:** Film language gives you distance and drama at once — trailer, slow-motion, deleted scene, costume designer, opening shot. “Narrate our first errand as if it secretly changed the whole film” turns the ordinary into the pivotal.
**Play it when:** When you want laughter + meaning without heaviness. Pair with Lore Drop — Director's is film, Lore is myth.
**Make it land:** Commit to the bit. Do the trailer voice. The silliness is what lets the sincere line land. **Watch out:** Don't let one partner's version erase the other's. Add a second commentary track.
**Expansion:**
- Give the blooper reel from our week a title and one scene that must stay in.
- If our relationship had a soundtrack cue for “they finally get it,” when would it play?
- Describe the scene where we *almost* missed each other. What saved it?
- What would the director's commentary say about a moment we thought was boring at the time?
**Twist — Split Screen:** Tell the same memory twice — once as a rom-com, once as a thriller. Which version is truer?

### House Rules — `house-rules` · prompt · Intensity 1
**What it really does:** World-builds for two. Inventing ridiculous rules (“blanket constitution”) is a safe way to negotiate real preferences — who gets the blanket, how Sundays work, what counts as a celebration — without a serious talk.
**Why the prompts work:** Absurdity lowers stakes. A rule about the last snack or emergency treats is really about fairness, generosity, and ritual. “Guests would find baffling but we would understand” is in-group identity in one prompt.
**Play it when:** Moving in, cohabiting, or just dreaming. Pair with Five-Minute Tradition — House Rules are the laws, Traditions are the rituals.
**Make it land:** Write the rule down. A house rule that stays spoken evaporates. **Watch out:** Don't smuggle a real complaint into a joke rule. If it's real, say it real later.
**Expansion:**
- Invent a house rule for what happens when one of us is grumpy and the other is not.
- What is the official policy for the “good” snack that is secretly for both of us?
- Write a house rule for phones at the table that we'd actually keep.
- Our house has a room that doesn't exist yet. What is its one rule?
**Twist — Rule Court:** After inventing 3 rules, each partner can veto one and amend one. The amended rule becomes canon.

### Growth Ring — `growth-ring` · journal · Intensity 2
**What it really does:** Creates longitudinal perspective. Couples forget progress because they live inside it. “Hard from 6 months ago that feels lighter now” makes growth visible without toxic positivity.
**Why the prompts work:** They are comparative, not absolute — better than *we used to*, not perfect. “Mistake that taught us something without becoming our whole story” and “difference understood with more kindness” are repair narratives.
**Play it when:** After a hard patch, or at a season change. Pair with Future Artifact — Growth Ring is what we survived, Artifact is what we keep.
**Make it land:** Be specific about the *before* and *after*. “We used to shut down, now we pause and return” is growth. “We've grown” is not. **Watch out:** Don't use growth to minimize a current hurt. Growth and still-hurting can both be true.
**Expansion:**
- What is something we used to argue about that we now handle with a shorthand?
- Name a small habit we built that future-us will thank us for.
- What would earlier-us be surprised to learn we can do now?
- Which growth are you proudest of that no one outside us would notice?
**Twist — Ring Count:** Draw concentric rings — one per chapter — and label each ring with one thing you learned. The center is now.

### Anchor Memory — `anchor-memory` · prompt · Intensity 2
**What it really does:** Co-regulation via memory. Replaying a safe memory slowly — sounds, smells, where each person sat — can calm the nervous system. It's not nostalgia; it's a portable safe room.
**Why the prompts work:** Sensory prompts (“only sounds and smells,” “where each sat”) slow the telling down. Slowness is the regulation. “We didn't have a plan but still felt okay” is the core anchor: safety without control.
**Play it when:** Anxious, overstimulated, or before sleep. Pair with Quiet Moment duel — Anchor is memory, Quiet Moment is breath.
**Make it land:** Tell it in present tense, slowly. “We are sitting…” not “We were sitting…” **Watch out:** Don't pick an anchor tied to a person/place that is now painful. Choose one that still feels safe.
**Expansion:**
- What is an anchor memory that is only 10 seconds long but still steadies you?
- Describe an anchor memory from *this month* — not years ago — that already feels safe.
- What anchor memory would you lend me on a day when I need one?
- If we could bottle one anchor memory as a scent or sound, which one?
**Twist — Anchor Swap:** Each partner tells the *other's* anchor memory as best they remember it. The corrections are tender.

### Unsent Draft — `unsent-draft` · prompt · Intensity 2
**What it really does:** Lowers the threshold for disclosure. The unsent text is the most honest text — it was written without performance. Giving it a home says: imperfect, unpolished messages count.
**Why the prompts work:** “Almost sent and deleted,” “thought about giving but didn't find the moment,” “if you didn't have to phrase it perfectly” — all remove the perfection filter. The prompts are small (a compliment, a funny observation, a queue text) so the risk is small.
**Play it when:** When communication feels formal or careful. Pair with Whisper Line — Unsent is the draft, Whisper is the delivery.
**Make it land:** Read it as written, typos and all. Don't polish it in the retelling. **Watch out:** Don't use this to deliver a delayed criticism. Keep it to the tender/funny/inviting drafts.
**Expansion:**
- What is a draft you wrote in your head while falling asleep last night?
- Share a draft that is just an emoji + 3 words. What did it mean?
- What is a message you *did* send that you wish you'd worded more gently?
- Write the draft you'd send if you knew I'd reply “tell me more.”
**Twist — Send It Now:** After sharing, actually send one unsent draft (or a cleaned-up version) before the night ends.

### Gratitude Snapshot — `gratitude-snapshot` · ritual · Intensity 1
**What it really does:** Present-moment attention, photographed. “Take a photo of your current view and caption it ‘this is where I love you from today’” turns a mundane view into a love letter. Async-friendly for distance.
**Why the prompts work:** They force you to look around — light, color, empty space, unnoticed corner — and connect it to your partner. “Before-and-after picture of a day your partner made better” is gratitude with a time-lapse.
**Play it when:** Apart, or in different rooms. Pair with Small Mercies — Snapshot is visual, Mercies is verbal.
**Make it land:** Actually take the photo. Don't just describe it. The photo is the ritual. **Watch out:** Don't curate for Instagram. The blurry, boring photo is more honest.
**Expansion:**
- Photograph the thing you're about to do next and caption it with why it made you think of me.
- Show me the view you wish I could see *right now*, not a beautiful view — your real one.
- Photograph something you touched today that reminded you of us.
- Take a photo of an empty chair and tell me who should be in it.
**Twist — Caption Swap:** Each partner captions the *other's* photo. Whose caption is more accurate?

### Shared Seed — `shared-seed` · ritual · Intensity 1 · No death, just droop
**What it really does:** Co-care without shame. The plant grows only if *both* water it. “No death, just droop” is the design ethic: missing a day doesn't punish you, it just asks you to return. That's habit design for real couples.
**Why the prompts work:** Gardening metaphors make patience speakable — watering, naming, harvesting, sign beside the seed. “What is already growing that we forget to notice?” reframes progress as existing, not missing.
**Play it when:** Daily, as a tiny check-in. Pair with Landing Lights — Seed is daytime care, Landing is nighttime arrival.
**Make it land:** Water it *together* in the app, then say one sentence about the intention. The tap + sentence is the ritual. **Watch out:** Don't weaponize droop. “You forgot to water” defeats the point.
**Expansion:**
- What is one seed we planted that is already sprouting, even if it's tiny?
- Name our plant. What does it need this week — more sun, more water, or just to be left alone?
- What would our garden look like if we only grew things that make us laugh?
- Design a seed for a habit we want to grow *for someone else*, not just us.
**Twist — Droop Day:** On a day the plant droops, each partner names one thing that drooped for them too, and one small watering they need.

### Rose, Thorn, Seed — `rose-thorn-seed` · prompt · Intensity 1
**What it really does:** Compact check-in with somewhere hopeful to land. Rose (bright) + Thorn (hard) + Seed (hope) prevents the check-in from becoming pure venting *or* pure positivity. The Seed forces a forward look.
**Why the prompts work:** Every prompt keeps the same 3-part grammar but changes the nouns — win/worry/intention, warm/tangled/grow, celebrate/care/make time for. That repetition makes it easy to do when tired. You don't have to invent a format, just fill it.
**Play it when:** Nightly, or as a weekly review. Pair with Weather Report — Rose/Thorn/Seed is events, Weather is feelings.
**Make it land:** One sentence each. Don't expand the Thorn into a full debrief unless both want to. **Watch out:** Don't fix the Thorn in the moment. Name it, then let the Seed be the next step.
**Expansion:**
- Rose: one thing you did for us. Thorn: one thing that felt heavy. Seed: one thing you want to try.
- What is our *shared* rose, thorn, and seed — not yours or mine, but ours?
- Give this *day* a rose, thorn, and seed in 9 words total.
- What seed from last time actually grew? Name it before planting a new one.
**Twist — Seed Only:** On a really hard day, skip Rose and Thorn. Just plant one Seed each. That's enough.

### Ordinary Treasure — `ordinary-treasure` · prompt · Intensity 1
**What it really does:** Sacralizes the ordinary. It fights the bias that only milestones matter by asking: which meal, sound, route, 5-minute memory would you freeze? The treasure is not the event — it's the attention.
**Why the prompts work:** “No special occasion,” “doing almost nothing together felt like exactly enough,” “ordinary object that carries more story than anyone would guess” — all reward noticing. The prompts are an argument: your life together is already full of treasure.
**Play it when:** When life feels boring or routine. Pair with Museum of Us — Treasure is the moment, Museum is the label.
**Make it land:** Pick one moment and stay there. Don't list five. Depth > breadth. **Watch out:** Don't compare to “real” adventures. The ordinary *is* the point.
**Expansion:**
- Which ordinary Tuesday from this month would you put in a treasure box?
- What is a treasured sound from our home that a stranger would ignore?
- Name a treasured *smell* from an ordinary day together.
- What ordinary treasure do you think *I* would pick? Were you right?
**Twist — Treasure Hunt:** Each partner hides a “treasure” (a note, a snack, a photo) in an ordinary place for the other to find tomorrow.

### Invisible Backpack — `invisible-backpack` · prompt · Intensity 2
**What it really does:** Makes mental load visible without demanding a fix. “What have you been carrying quietly?” invites context, not a to-do list. The care offered is *one notch lighter*, not “let me solve your life.”
**Why the prompts work:** Backpack metaphors — carrying, shelf, checklist, weight, 10% less stretched — make invisible labor tangible. “Responsibility you'd like me to understand better without needing me to fix it” is the key distinction: understanding *is* the care.
**Play it when:** Busy, overwhelmed, or when one partner seems distant. Pair with Comfort Menu — Backpack names the load, Menu names the help.
**Make it land:** Ask “Do you want company, help, or just to be heard?” before offering a solution. **Watch out:** Don't turn it into a competition over who carries more. Both backpacks are real.
**Expansion:**
- What is one thing in your backpack that you didn't choose to carry?
- If we could set down one backpack item together for a week, which one?
- What is a small, invisible task I do that you don't realize I carry?
- Describe what your backpack feels like today — heavy, lopsided, zipped shut, overflowing?
**Twist — Unpack One:** Each partner picks one item to unpack out loud, and the other can only say: “Thank you for telling me. What would make it lighter?”

### Gentle Signal — `gentle-signal` · ritual · Intensity 1
**What it really does:** Pre-negotiates repair language. The hardest moment to find words is *during* disconnection. Inventing a phrase/gesture *now* — “I want to reconnect but don't have perfect words” — gives you a tool for later.
**Why the prompts work:** Every prompt is a signal for a hard moment: pause, overwhelm, need for company, return to unfinished conversation, yes/later/not today. The “yes, later, or not today” prompt is consent design: a check-in that allows a no.
**Play it when:** Calm, not mid-conflict. Pair with Invisible Backpack — Signal is the phrase, Backpack is the load.
**Make it land:** Actually agree on one signal and use it this week. A signal you never use is just a nice idea. **Watch out:** Don't invent a signal that is actually a shutdown (“Leave me alone” with no return time). Signals need a return.
**Expansion:**
- Invent a signal for “I'm not upset with you, I'm just tired.”
- What signal means “I need a hug but I can't ask for one out loud”?
- Create a signal for “This conversation matters, can we finish it tomorrow?”
- What is a signal *I* use that you wish I'd explain better?
**Twist — Signal Test:** Use your new signal once this week in a low-stakes moment, just to practice.

### Five-Minute Tradition — `five-minute-tradition` · ritual · Intensity 1
**What it really does:** Turns affection into a repeatable ritual. Five minutes is small enough to actually do. Naming it + one rule makes it *yours* — a tradition is just a repeated moment with a name.
**Why the prompts work:** They cover every context — reunion, chore, rainy day, message, Sunday, song, doorway, 1-minute version. The “smaller version for days when we have only one minute” prompt is habit-design wisdom: a tradition that can't shrink will die.
**Play it when:** When you want closeness but have no time. Pair with House Rules — Traditions are rituals, Rules are laws.
**Make it land:** Pick *one* tradition and do it tomorrow. Don't design five and do none. **Watch out:** Don't make it a performance. If it feels like homework, shrink it.
**Expansion:**
- Design a 5-minute tradition for the moment we both walk in the door.
- Invent a tradition that is just a sentence we say to each other every night.
- What 5-minute tradition would our future selves thank us for starting now?

**Why the prompts work:** The 56-question bank is all *shared* choices — mascot, tiny luxury, rainy afternoon, dream home — so a match feels like “we're on the same wavelength” and a mismatch feels like “oh, I didn't know you liked that.” Both are useful.
**Play it when:** As a warm-up. Pair with Same Wavelength — Mind Meld is match, Same Wavelength is sealed comparison.
**Make it land:** Don't defend your pick. Ask “Why did you pick that?” **Watch out:** Don't keep score across nights. One match is not compatibility.
**Expansion:** See Expanded Mind Meld bank in §5 — 12 new shared-choice questions.
**Twist — Meld Bet:** Before revealing, each partner bets: “I think we matched / didn't match.” The bet is the fun.

### The Prediction Box — `prediction-box` · prediction · Intensity 1
**What it really does:** One player predicts the answer *before* the other chooses honestly. It's theory of mind as a game: do you know your partner well enough to predict them? The prediction is the compliment — “I pay attention to you.”

## Board activities — Spicy (22)

The Spicy set is often misread as “heat.” It's not. It's *closeness with consent built in*. Every activity here is about tiny, specific, opt-in affection — a look, a touch, a whisper, a detail — not explicit content. The design rule across all 22 is: **make the invitation clear, make the pass equally clear, and let specificity do the work.**

### Replay — `replay-favorite-kiss` · prompt · Intensity 1
**What it really does:** Memory that is intimate without being explicit. Replaying a kiss via memory — what happened before/after, the place, the song, the weather — lets you savor without performing. It's nostalgia as intimacy.
**Why the prompts work:** “Not the most romantic one, but the one when…”, “almost-kiss,” “goodbye that felt sweet,” “ordinary hello turned into unhurried reunion” — all reward *specific* kisses, not generic ones. The prompts ask for context (place, anticipation, song) because context is what makes a memory feel real.
**Play it when:** Tender + a little flirty. Pair with Rewind — Replay is kisses, Rewind is touches.
**Make it land:** Tell it slowly, in present tense. Let the memory arrive. **Watch out:** Don't compare kisses. Each replay is its own moment.
**Expansion:**
- Describe a kiss that was funny — we laughed before, during, or after.
- What is a kiss you *wish* you could replay, but the moment is gone?
- Describe a kiss from a version of us that no longer exists — what would you tell them?
- What would a replay of *today's* kiss look like if we filmed it in slow motion?
**Twist — Replay Director:** After telling, your partner directs a 10-second “replay” (a look, a hand squeeze, a hug) — not a kiss, just the feeling.

## Duel activity catalog — expanded

Each duel below keeps its original mechanics and adds: what it *teaches*, how it pairs with board activities, and a variant.

### Star Map — `constellation-home` · tender · Intensity 1 · Selectable
**Teaches:** Values are not fixed. Ranking five values privately, then comparing, shows *where you are now*, not who you are forever. The reveal is not “we match / don't match” — it's “tell me why you put Trust above Laughter this week.”
**Pairs with:** Wavelength (both rank/guess), Future Artifact (values as objects).
**Variant — Re-rank in a Month:** Do Star Map once, then re-rank the same five values a month later. What moved, and why?

### Heartbeat Hold — `heartbeat-hold-tender` · tender · Intensity 1 · Registered, not selectable
**Teaches:** Co-regulation. Holding a button together while the board pulses is *staying* — not fixing, not talking, just staying. Release ends it. The game ends when one lets go, and that's okay. Duration is not a score.
**Pairs with:** Quiet Moment, Harmonic Lock, Tempo Tap — all non-verbal attunement.
**Variant — Hold + Breath:** Hold the button and breathe together for 3 breaths. Let go on the exhale.

### Letterpress — `letterpress-one-word` · tender · Intensity 2 · Selectable
**Teaches:** Co-authorship. You each add four words to a starter, the reveal weaves them alternating. You have to *read* the starter and imagine your partner's words. The sentence you get is neither yours nor theirs — it's *ours*.
**Pairs with:** Our Next Line (both are co-writing), Word Weaver (both are wordplay).
**Variant — Letterpress Reply:** After the reveal, each partner writes a one-word reply to the woven sentence.

### Quiet Moment — `sync-breath` · tender · Intensity 1 · Selectable
**Teaches:** Choice in regulation. You choose a breathing, grounding, or muscle-release pause. Choice is the mechanic — you pick what you need, not what the game tells you to need.

### Gratitude Duel — `gratitude-duel` · tender · Intensity 1 · Selectable

## Guardrails — what not to add

The expanded catalog adds 12 activities and ~450 new prompts. It deliberately does *not* add:
- **Explicit content.** The Spicy set stays at tiny, specific, opt-in closeness. Explicitness would break the game's core bias: specificity > heat, consent > pressure.
- **Scoring / leaderboards.** No activity should rank couples against each other. The only “scores” are: did we match? did we guess? did we stay? Those are conversations, not rankings.
- **Advice / therapy.** Bad Advice is a joke, not therapy. Repair Kit is a language, not a treatment. The game gives you *words*, not *diagnoses*.
- **More moods.** Cozy / Playful / Flirty / Dreamy / Wild already cover the range. New moods would fragment the catalog without adding depth.
- **More “big” prompts.** The catalog's strength is *small* prompts. A prompt that asks for a lifetime is less useful than one that asks for *today*. The expansions above keep that rule: every new prompt is small, recent, and specific.
